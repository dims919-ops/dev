# Legal Task Mapping for GenAI Use

This file maps typical legal and institutional tasks and assesses where Generative AI could support work, where it creates risk, and where human judgment must remain central.

## 1. Main legal work tasks

| Task | Description | Frequency | Document-heavy? | GenAI potential |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Legal research | Searching laws, case law, institutional acts, and legal opinions | High | Yes | Medium |
| Drafting legal opinions | Preparing formal legal reasoning and institutional responses | High | Yes | Medium |
| Reviewing documents | Checking contracts, submissions, evidence, correspondence, and annexes | High | Yes | High |
| Summarising cases | Preparing summaries of disputes, court decisions, or arbitration developments | High | Yes | High |
| Preparing government information notes | Structuring information for Government sessions or internal decision-making | Medium | Yes | High |
| Coordinating with institutions | Communication with ministries, State Attorney, courts, agencies, and commissions | High | Yes | Medium |
| Risk assessment | Identifying legal, procedural, financial, and reputational risks | Medium | Partly | Medium |
| Decision support | Preparing options, consequences, and recommendations for senior officials | Medium | Yes | Medium |

## 2. Where GenAI can help

GenAI can realistically support legal and institutional work in tasks that are repetitive, text-heavy, and require structuring or summarising large volumes of material.

Possible use cases:

- summarising long documents
- preparing first drafts of formal correspondence
- comparing versions of legal texts
- extracting key facts from case files
- creating chronologies
- preparing meeting notes
- drafting interview questions
- creating risk matrices
- translating or improving legal-administrative language
- preparing internal briefing notes

## 3. Where GenAI is risky

GenAI should be used carefully or avoided in tasks involving final legal judgment, confidential materials, procedural deadlines, sensitive evidence, or official institutional responsibility.

High-risk areas:

- final legal opinions
- litigation strategy
- arbitration defence strategy
- privileged or confidential documents
- personal data
- classified or politically sensitive information
- direct reliance on generated case law or legal citations
- official decisions without human verification

## 4. Human judgment requirements

Human professionals must remain responsible for:

- legal interpretation
- final verification of sources
- procedural compliance
- ethical responsibility
- confidentiality assessment
- institutional accountability
- communication with courts, ministries, and senior officials
- strategic decisions in disputes and negotiations

## 5. Preliminary research insight

The most realistic role of GenAI in legal work is not to replace lawyers or legal officers, but to support task preparation, document structuring, summarisation, and first-draft production. The central organisational issue is therefore not only technical adoption, but governance: who may use GenAI, for which tasks, under what safeguards, and with what level of human review.
